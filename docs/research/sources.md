# Pronunciation & Zhuyin Research

Research notes for the French Letter Pronunciation Trainer, covering all 38 letter ids returned by
`Layout.getAllLetterIds()` in `js/layout.js` (the 26 base AZERTY letters plus the 12 accented
letters/dead-key combinations: é, è, ç, à, ù, â, ê, î, ô, û, ï, ë).

Sourcing priority follows the task brief: (1) FLE (Français Langue Étrangère) pedagogical resources
aimed at learners, (2) genuine Taiwanese French-pedagogy material specifically for the zhuyin
(bopomofo) comparisons, (3) general academic French-phonology references only to fill articulatory
detail gaps. Where no genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy source could be found for a zhuyin comparison, this
is stated explicitly rather than inventing one.

Key Taiwanese/Chinese-language sources used repeatedly below:

- **abconline.com.tw** — blog of 巨匠美語 (Gjun / PCSchool Group), an established Taiwanese language-school
  chain; gives an explicit letter-by-letter zhuyin comparison table for French vowels, nasal vowels,
  consonants and semivowels.
- **emilieyo.com** / **french-nautilus.com** (法語鸚鵡螺) — a Taiwanese French-pronunciation course and
  its companion blog, written by "Emily," a Taiwan-based, DALF C1-certified French speaker; the course
  page dedicated to the 12 French diacritics is the single most directly relevant Taiwanese source for
  this task's accented-letter section.
- **blog.wordup.com.tw** (WORD UP 聰明學習) — a Taiwanese exam-prep/language blog with a French-alphabet
  article giving zhuyin comparisons.
- **ai.glossika.com/zh-tw** — Glossika Taiwan's blog, a Taiwan-based language-learning company; detailed
  FLE-style pronunciation rules (not zhuyin-based, used for articulatory/rule facts).
- **lecoqfr.com** (Le Coq Français) — a Chinese-language (Hong Kong-based, not Taiwan-specific) FLE blog,
  used only to corroborate the aspiration-vs-voicing explanation for why several consonants have no
  clean zhuyin equivalent; flagged as non-Taiwanese where cited.

## a — A

- IPA: /a/ (letter name = the vowel sound itself)
- Sound description: Low front unrounded vowel. The mouth opens wide, the tongue lies low and forward, and the lips stay relaxed/neutral (not rounded). Voiced, oral (non-nasal), continuous airflow through an open, unobstructed vocal tract.
- Zhuyin: ㄚ
- Zhuyin source note: Genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy match, cross-confirmed by two independent sources: 巨匠美語/abconline.com.tw gives "[a] 唸作「ㄚ」，類似「啊」" and WORD UP's blog independently states "字母「a」跟注音「ㄚ」一樣." Both are open, unrounded low vowels, so the match is close.
- Example word(s): la, papa
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門：法語字母與音標教學 (abconline.com.tw / 巨匠美語) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 原來法文發音這麼多眉角！(WORD UP 聰明學習) — https://blog.wordup.com.tw/blog/2021/10/25/fr-alphabet-pronunciation/

## z — Z

- IPA: /zɛd/ (letter name); the letter represents /z/
- Sound description: Voiced alveolar fricative — same tongue position as s (tip near the ridge behind the upper front teeth, air hissing through a narrow gap), but with the vocal cords vibrating continuously throughout.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: Standard Mandarin/zhuyin has no phonemically voiced fricative series at all — its ㄗ symbol is actually a voiceless *unaspirated affricate* [ts], a different manner and voicing from French /z/, so it is not a genuine match despite the superficial letter-shape resemblance. abconline.com.tw lists z among the sounds "其發音方式與英文相同" (pronounced the same way as in English), which is an English-based comparison, not a claim of a Mandarin/zhuyin equivalent, confirmed by Wikipedia's description of Mandarin's aspiration-based (not voicing-based) consonant contrasts.
- Example word(s): zéro, gaz
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - Standard Chinese phonology — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese_phonology

## e — E

- IPA: /ə/ (letter name pronunciation — schwa)
- Sound description: Mid-central vowel (schwa). The tongue rests in a neutral, relaxed mid-central position, the lips are loosely and slightly rounded, and the mouth is barely open. Voiced, oral, said lightly and briefly — it often drops out entirely in casual speech (e.g. "le" → "l'").
- Zhuyin: ㄜ
- Zhuyin source note: Genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy match confirmed by three independent Taiwanese sources: abconline.com.tw ("[ə] 唸作「ㄜ」"), emilieyo.com/French Nautilus ("e [ə] → ㄜ", cheval), and WORD UP's blog ("e 唸 ㄜ...輕輕地、放鬆地唸" — pronounced lightly and relaxed). All three independently agree.
- Example word(s): le, petit
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 【學法語】法文基礎發音 (Emily YO / 法語鸚鵡螺) — https://emilieyo.com/archives/9086

## r — R

- IPA: /ɛʁ/ (letter name); the letter represents /ʁ/
- Sound description: Voiced uvular fricative (sometimes realized as an approximant). The back of the tongue rises toward the uvula at the very back of the throat, and voiced air rubs through the narrow gap there — a "gargling" quality very different from the English or Mandarin r, both made further forward with the tongue tip/blade.
- Zhuyin: Closest approximation ㄏ (as in "喝" hē)
- Zhuyin source note: Genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy match, cross-confirmed by two independent Taiwanese sources: WORD UP's blog describes it as "唸 ㄏㄜ...有點像中文的「喝」" and abconline.com.tw independently describes it as "深喉音，類似「喝」的輕聲" (a deep throat sound, similar to a soft "喝"). Both sources explicitly flag this as only a rough approximation — French /ʁ/ is a continuous uvular fricative, further back and more constricted than Mandarin's velar/glottal ㄏ.
- Example word(s): rouge, Paris
- Sources:
  - 原來法文發音這麼多眉角！(WORD UP 聰明學習) — https://blog.wordup.com.tw/blog/2021/10/25/fr-alphabet-pronunciation/
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - La consonne [R] (Français Interactif, University of Texas at Austin) — https://laits.utexas.edu/fi/html/pho/04.html

## t — T

- IPA: /te/ (letter name); the letter represents /t/
- Sound description: Voiceless alveolar stop. The tongue tip presses against the ridge behind the upper front teeth, blocking airflow, then releases without vocal cord vibration; like p and k, French t is never puffed with a strong burst of air (unaspirated), unlike English t.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: Same voicing-vs-aspiration mismatch as p and k below. Zhuyin's ㄉ/ㄊ pair contrasts by aspiration (unaspirated vs aspirated), not by voicing as French t/d does, so neither symbol is a genuine match. abconline.com.tw (a Taiwanese source) explicitly flags [t] (with b, d, p, k, g) as one of the sounds hardest for Chinese-speaking learners to place correctly, for exactly this reason; Le Coq Français's Chinese-language FLE blog (Hong Kong-based, not Taiwan-specific) gives a detailed explanation of the same underlying mismatch.
- Example word(s): tu, table
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 法文常見發音問題（五）：清、濁輔音，送氣不送氣？(Le Coq Français) — https://www.lecoqfr.com/%E6%B3%95%E6%96%87%E5%B8%B8%E8%A6%8B%E7%99%BC%E9%9F%B3%E5%95%8F%E9%A1%8C%EF%BC%88%E4%BA%94%EF%BC%89%EF%BC%9A%E6%B8%85%E3%80%81%E6%BF%81%E8%BC%94%E9%9F%B3%EF%BC%8C%E9%80%81%E6%B0%A3%E4%B8%8D%E9%80%81/

## y — Y

- IPA: /i ɡʁɛk/ (letter name, "i grec" = "Greek i"); within words the letter represents /i/ (vowel, e.g. "stylo") or /j/ (semivowel before a vowel, e.g. "yaourt")
- Sound description: The letter's name is two syllables — /i/ (as in the letter i) followed by /ɡʁɛk/ (hard g, uvular r, closed e, voiceless k). As a sound within words, y is either the same high front unrounded vowel as i, or, right before another vowel, a fast glide from that same tongue position (semivowel) — voiced and oral throughout either way.
- Zhuyin: ㄧ (for the plain vowel use; see note for the semivowel use)
- Zhuyin source note: For the plain vowel use of y (as in "stylo"), Taiwanese sources treat it identically to i: a Taiwanese-audience search summary attributed to 法語星球/Planète du français states "法文中的i和y兩個字母發音，跟注音「一」是完全一樣的." For the semivowel /j/ use (as in "yeux"), abconline.com.tw gives "音似「ㄧ」、「ㄜ」連起來唸的聲音" (sounds like ㄧ and ㄜ said quickly together). No Taiwanese source was found giving a zhuyin equivalent for the full two-syllable letter *name* "i grec" itself, so that part is not claimed as sourced.
- Example word(s): stylo, yaourt
- Sources:
  - 8.2: The French Y and I (Palomar College, Humanities LibreTexts FREN 140) — https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/Palomar_College/FREN_140:_Basic_French_Pronunciation/08:_Simple_Vowels/8.02:_The_French_Y_and_I
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## u — U

- IPA: /y/
- Sound description: High front rounded vowel — round the lips as for "ou" while keeping the tongue forward as for "i." No equivalent exists in English or Mandarin. Voiced, oral, continuous.
- Zhuyin: ㄩ
- Zhuyin source note: One of the most consistently cited comparisons in Taiwanese French-teaching material — independently confirmed by abconline.com.tw ("[y] 唸作「ㄩ」"), WORD UP's blog ("u 唸 ㄩ"), and emilieyo.com/French Nautilus ("u [y] → ㄩ", tu). Both sounds are high front rounded vowels, so the match is close.
- Example word(s): tu, lune
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 原來法文發音這麼多眉角！(WORD UP 聰明學習) — https://blog.wordup.com.tw/blog/2021/10/25/fr-alphabet-pronunciation/
  - 【學法語】法文基礎發音 (emilieyo.com) — https://emilieyo.com/archives/9086

## i — I

- IPA: /i/
- Sound description: High front unrounded vowel. The tongue is pushed high and forward, close to the palate, and the lips are spread/unrounded as in a slight smile. Voiced, oral, continuous.
- Zhuyin: ㄧ
- Zhuyin source note: Direct genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy match, confirmed independently by abconline.com.tw ("[i] 唸作「ㄧ」，但較短促" — pronounced ㄧ, but shorter/crisper) and emilieyo.com ("i [i] → 一"). Both are high front unrounded vowels.
- Example word(s): il, midi
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 【學法語】法文基礎發音 (emilieyo.com) — https://emilieyo.com/archives/9086

## o — O

- IPA: /o/ (closed o)
- Sound description: Mid-back rounded vowel. The lips push forward and round, and the tongue rises toward the back of the mouth at mid-height. Voiced, oral, continuous.
- Zhuyin: ㄛ
- Zhuyin source note: Genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy source — abconline.com.tw explicitly compares the closed [o] (using "hôtel," "métro" as examples) to ㄛ produced with rounded, puckered lips. A different Taiwanese source (emilieyo.com/French Nautilus) instead compares a more open "o" allophone (as in "rose," "pomme") to ㄡ; that is noted here as an alternate view, since zhuyin has no symbol that cleanly separates French's closed/open o contrast.
- Example word(s): mot, vélo
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 【學法語】法文基礎發音 (emilieyo.com) — https://emilieyo.com/archives/9086

## p — P

- IPA: /pe/ (letter name); the letter represents /p/
- Sound description: Voiceless bilabial stop — both lips press together, blocking airflow, then release without vocal cord vibration. Unlike English, French p carries no strong puff of air (unaspirated).
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: Because French p is unaspirated, general Chinese-language phonetics discussion notes it can sound closer to Mandarin's unaspirated ㄅ than to aspirated ㄆ — but this specific pairing was found only via general/non-Taiwan-specific phonetics discussion, not a Taiwan-specific FLE resource, and offering either zhuyin symbol risks confusing learners about voicing vs. aspiration, so no equivalent is claimed. abconline.com.tw (Taiwanese) flags p, along with b, d, t, g, k, as the six sounds hardest for Chinese speakers to place correctly, precisely because Mandarin's stop contrast is aspiration-based while French's is voicing-based.
- Example word(s): père, papa
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 法文常見發音問題（五）：清、濁輔音，送氣不送氣？(Le Coq Français) — https://www.lecoqfr.com/%E6%B3%95%E6%96%87%E5%B8%B8%E8%A6%8B%E7%99%BC%E9%9F%B3%E5%95%8F%E9%A1%8C%EF%BC%88%E4%BA%94%EF%BC%89%EF%BC%9A%E6%B8%85%E3%80%81%E6%BF%81%E8%BC%94%E9%9F%B3%EF%BC%8C%E9%80%81%E6%B0%A3%E4%B8%8D%E9%80%81/

## q — Q

- IPA: /ky/ (letter name); the letter represents /k/ (almost always appearing as "qu")
- Sound description: The same voiceless velar stop as k — back of the tongue against the soft palate, no vocal cord vibration. In French, q almost always appears as "qu," pronounced /k/ with the u silent (unlike English "qu" = /kw/).
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent (same reasoning as k)
- Zhuyin source note: Same aspiration-vs-voicing mismatch as k (see below). Glossika Taiwan's guide separately notes that qu/cqu are always /k/ in French, never /kw/ — a common false-friend for Chinese speakers coming from English spelling habits, though not a zhuyin-comparison claim.
- Example word(s): qui, cinq
- Sources:
  - 從零開始學法文，一次掌握法文的基礎發音規則！(Glossika Taiwan) — https://ai.glossika.com/zh-tw/blog/intro-series-french-pronunciation
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## s — S

- IPA: /ɛs/ (letter name); the letter represents /s/, or /z/ between two vowels
- Sound description: Voiceless alveolar fricative — the tongue tip approaches the ridge behind the upper front teeth, letting air hiss through a narrow channel, with no vocal cord vibration. Between two vowels, a single s instead becomes /z/ (voiced) at the same tongue position.
- Zhuyin: ㄙ (for the voiceless /s/ value only)
- Zhuyin source note: abconline.com.tw groups s with f, v, l, m, n as pronounced "其發音方式與英文相同" (the same way as in English), and ㄙ is the everyday Mandarin voiceless alveolar fricative used as the comparison. No equivalent is claimed for the voiced /z/ allophone (see the "z" entry).
- Example word(s): sel, maison (the s in maison is the voiced /z/ allophone)
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 從零開始學法文 (Glossika Taiwan) — https://ai.glossika.com/zh-tw/blog/intro-series-french-pronunciation

## d — D

- IPA: /de/ (letter name); the letter represents /d/
- Sound description: Voiced alveolar stop. The tongue tip presses against the ridge behind the upper front teeth, blocking airflow, then releases; the vocal cords vibrate throughout.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: Same voicing-vs-aspiration mismatch as b/g/p/t/k — zhuyin's ㄉ is voiceless and unaspirated, not voiced, so it is not claimed as a genuine equivalent. Sourced to abconline.com.tw's explicit statement that b/d/g/p/k/t are hard for Chinese-speaking learners because Mandarin has no phonemic voicing contrast, and to Wikipedia's description of Mandarin's aspiration-based stop contrasts.
- Example word(s): dans, madame
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - Standard Chinese phonology — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese_phonology

## f — F

- IPA: /ɛf/ (letter name); the letter represents /f/
- Sound description: Voiceless labiodental fricative. The upper teeth touch the lower lip lightly, and air hisses through the narrow gap; the vocal cords do not vibrate.
- Zhuyin: ㄈ
- Zhuyin source note: abconline.com.tw's Taiwanese-pedagogy guide states plainly that [f] (along with v, s, z, l, m, n) is "其發音方式與英文相同" (pronounced the same way as in English); ㄈ is the standard bopomofo labiodental fricative and the everyday Mandarin comparison for this sound, and French/Mandarin f share no voicing-contrast complication.
- Example word(s): femme, neuf
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## g — G

- IPA: /ʒe/ (letter name); the letter represents /g/ before a, o, u (or l, r), or /ʒ/ before e, i, y
- Sound description: Two sounds depending on context. Before a, o, u (or l, r), g is a voiced velar stop /g/ — the back of the tongue presses against the soft palate, blocking then releasing air, with voicing. Before e, i, y, g softens to /ʒ/, a voiced postalveolar fricative (like the middle sound in English "measure") — the tongue approaches just behind the alveolar ridge, letting voiced air hiss through.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: The hard-g/soft-g rule is sourced to Glossika Taiwan's FLE guide ("g 的發音為 /ʒ/ 或 /g/... 位在 e、i 或 y 前面的 g 發音為 /ʒ/"). The hard /g/ value has the same voicing mismatch as b/d (see those entries). abconline.com.tw compares the soft /ʒ/ value to the "s" sound in English "television," not to a zhuyin symbol; no Taiwanese source was found proposing a zhuyin symbol for either value of g, so none is invented here.
- Example word(s): gare (hard g), âge (soft g)
- Sources:
  - 從零開始學法文 (Glossika Taiwan) — https://ai.glossika.com/zh-tw/blog/intro-series-french-pronunciation
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## h — H

- IPA: /aʃ/ (letter name); the letter represents no sound (h is always silent in modern standard French)
- Sound description: The letter's name is pronounced /aʃ/ (open "a" + "sh"), but h itself is never pronounced as a sound within words — it is a purely orthographic letter. There are two invisible "flavors": *h muet* ("mute h," behaves like a vowel, allows elision/liaison, e.g. l'hôtel) and *h aspiré* ("aspirated h," behaves like a consonant, blocks elision/liaison, e.g. le haricot) — but even h aspiré carries no actual airflow or aspiration; the name is historical and can be misleading.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent (there is no sound to map, since h is always silent in French)
- Zhuyin source note: This is a property of French itself rather than a sourcing gap — h has no phonetic realization at all. Confirmed by francaisavecpierre.com's dedicated FLE explainer and by Glossika Taiwan's guide ("h 在法文不發音").
- Example word(s): hôtel (h muet), haricot (h aspiré)
- Sources:
  - H aspiré et H muet (francaisavecpierre.com) — https://www.francaisavecpierre.com/h-aspire-et-h-muet/
  - 從零開始學法文 (Glossika Taiwan) — https://ai.glossika.com/zh-tw/blog/intro-series-french-pronunciation

## j — J

- IPA: /ʒi/ (letter name); the letter represents /ʒ/
- Sound description: Voiced postalveolar fricative — the tongue approaches just behind the alveolar ridge (further back than for s/z), lips slightly rounded/protruded, and voiced air hisses through continuously (like the middle sound in English "measure" or "television").
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: abconline.com.tw compares /ʒ/ to the "s" sound in the English word "television" rather than to any Mandarin/zhuyin sound, and no Taiwanese source was found offering a zhuyin symbol for it. Mandarin has no voiced fricative at this place of articulation (its closest sound, ㄖ, is a very different retroflex approximant), so none is invented here.
- Example word(s): jour, bonjour
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - 從零開始學法文 (Glossika Taiwan) — https://ai.glossika.com/zh-tw/blog/intro-series-french-pronunciation

## k — K

- IPA: /ka/ (letter name); the letter represents /k/
- Sound description: Voiceless velar stop — the back of the tongue presses against the soft palate, blocking airflow, then releases without vocal cord vibration.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: Naively matching French's unaspirated voiceless /k/ to zhuyin's aspirated ㄎ would be misleading, since Mandarin's ㄎ/ㄍ pair contrasts by aspiration, not by French's voicing contrast. No Taiwanese-pedagogy source was found proposing a zhuyin symbol for this letter, so none is claimed; see abconline.com.tw and Wikipedia's Standard Chinese phonology article for the underlying mismatch.
- Example word(s): kilo, ski
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols
  - Standard Chinese phonology — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese_phonology

## l — L

- IPA: /ɛl/ (letter name); the letter represents /l/
- Sound description: Voiced alveolar lateral approximant — the tongue tip touches the ridge behind the upper front teeth while air flows around the sides of the tongue; the vocal cords vibrate throughout.
- Zhuyin: ㄌ
- Zhuyin source note: abconline.com.tw's Taiwanese guide groups l with f, v, s, z, m, n as pronounced "the same way as in English," and ㄌ is the everyday Mandarin approximation for this sound in both English and French.
- Example word(s): lune, elle
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## m — M

- IPA: /ɛm/ (letter name); the letter represents /m/
- Sound description: Voiced bilabial nasal — the lips close completely while air flows out through the nose instead of the mouth; the vocal cords vibrate.
- Zhuyin: ㄇ
- Zhuyin source note: Same source group as l — abconline.com.tw states m is pronounced the same way in French as in English, and ㄇ is the standard Mandarin bilabial nasal used as the everyday comparison.
- Example word(s): mère, femme
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## u-grave — Ù

- IPA: /u/ (identical to the "ou" sound)
- Sound description: Same high back rounded vowel as the digraph "ou" — lips tightly rounded and pushed forward, tongue raised toward the back. The grave accent changes no sound at all; ù exists in only one common French word, "où" ("where"), purely to distinguish it in writing from "ou" ("or").
- Zhuyin: ㄨ (same as "ou," since the sound is identical)
- Zhuyin source note: Sourced to Glossika Taiwan's guide, which explicitly groups "ou、où、oû 的發音為 /u/" (ou, où, oû are all pronounced /u/), and to emilieyo.com's/WORD UP's independent comparison of the "ou" sound to ㄨ. Français Interactif (UT Austin) separately confirms that the grave accent on letters other than e "does not indicate a sound difference" and exists only to distinguish homophones. Since ù never changes the sound from plain "ou," the already-sourced ㄨ mapping is reused here rather than claimed as a distinct new finding.
- Example word(s): où
- Sources:

## w — W

- IPA: /dubləve/ ("double vé," letter name); the letter represents /w/ in English-origin loanwords (e.g. "week-end") or /v/ in Germanic-origin loanwords (e.g. "wagon")
- Sound description: A rare, non-native letter used only in loanwords. As /w/, it is a voiced labio-velar glide — lips rounded as for "ou," tongue raised toward the back, gliding quickly into the next vowel (same as English "w"). As /v/, it is identical to the letter v's sound.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: No Taiwanese-pedagogy source was found offering a zhuyin symbol for w specifically; abconline.com.tw simply states its /w/ value is "與英文相同" (the same as English) without giving a zhuyin comparison, so none is invented here.
- Example word(s): week-end, wagon

## x — X

- IPA: /iks/ (letter name); the letter represents /ks/, /gz/, /s/, /z/, or is silent, depending on the word
- Sound description: No single sound — x's pronunciation depends entirely on position: /ks/ before a consonant or at the end of most words ("taxi," "texte"); /gz/ at the start of a word or between vowels, especially in "ex-" ("exemple," "examen"); /s/ in a few number words in isolation ("six," "dix"); /z/ in liaison or in "deuxième," "sixième"; and silent in some words ("choix," "prix").
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: No Taiwanese-pedagogy source was found proposing a zhuyin equivalent for x — the letter represents too many different sounds/clusters to map to one symbol, and zhuyin syllables cannot represent consonant clusters like /ks/ or /gz/ at all. The sound-per-context breakdown is drawn from general FLE material.
- Example word(s): taxi, exemple

## c — C

- IPA: /se/ (letter name); the letter represents /k/ before a, o, u (or a consonant), or /s/ before e, i, y
- Sound description: Two different consonant sounds depending on context. Before a, o, u (or a consonant), c is a voiceless velar stop /k/ — the back of the tongue presses against the soft palate, blocking air, then releases. Before e, i, y, c softens to /s/, a voiceless alveolar fricative — the tongue tip approaches the ridge behind the upper teeth, letting air hiss through a narrow gap.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent (the letter represents two different sounds, so no single symbol applies)
- Zhuyin source note: The hard/soft rule itself ("Cédille devant A, O, U; sinon, c'est tout nu") is well documented by general French-orthography references. No Taiwanese source was found giving one zhuyin symbol for "c" as a letter, understandably, since it is not one sound; the /s/-context value is reasonably close to ㄙ (see "s"), and the /k/-context value carries the same aspiration-vs-voicing mismatch as "k" (see that entry), so neither is asserted here as the letter's single equivalent.
- Example word(s): café (hard c), cinéma (soft c)
- Sources:
  - La cédille et le c dur : règle simple pour ne plus se tromper (lorthographe.fr) — https://www.lorthographe.fr/blog/la-cedille-et-le-c-dur-la-regle-simple-pour-ne-plus-se-tromper
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## v — V

- IPA: /ve/ (letter name); the letter represents /v/
- Sound description: Voiced labiodental fricative — the upper teeth touch the lower lip lightly (same mouth position as f), and voiced air hisses through continuously.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: Mandarin/zhuyin has no voiced-fricative series at all — there is no symbol corresponding to /v/. abconline.com.tw's Taiwanese guide lists v among the sounds "pronounced the same as in English," but this reflects an English-based comparison, not a Mandarin/zhuyin equivalent, since Mandarin has no /v/ phoneme either.
- Example word(s): vous, voiture

## b — B

- IPA: /be/ (letter name); the letter represents /b/
- Sound description: Voiced bilabial stop/plosive. Both lips press together then release, with the vocal cords vibrating throughout (voicing starts before the lips even open). Airflow is fully blocked momentarily, then released in a short burst.
- Zhuyin: No close equivalent
- Zhuyin source note: Mandarin/zhuyin does not have a phonemically voiced stop series — ㄅ is a voiceless unaspirated stop, contrasting with aspirated ㄆ, whereas French contrasts b/p purely by vocal-cord voicing. abconline.com.tw's Taiwanese-audience guide explicitly flags [b] (with p, d, t, g, k) as one of the hardest sound groups for Chinese speakers, precisely because "舌頭位置、嘴型完全一樣，只能用聲帶振動與否去判別" (tongue position and mouth shape are identical; only vocal-cord vibration differs). Wikipedia's Standard Chinese phonology article confirms Mandarin's stop series contrasts by aspiration, not voicing, so no zhuyin equivalent is claimed.
- Example word(s): bon, robe
- Sources:
  - 法文音標發音入門 (abconline.com.tw) — https://www.abconline.com.tw/blog/french/learning-phonetic-symbols

## n — N

- IPA: /ɛn/ (letter name); the letter represents /n/
- Sound description: Voiced alveolar nasal — the tongue tip touches the ridge behind the upper front teeth while air flows out through the nose; the vocal cords vibrate.
- Zhuyin: ㄋ
- Zhuyin source note: Same source group as l/m — abconline.com.tw states n is pronounced the same way in French as in English; ㄋ is the standard Mandarin alveolar nasal comparison.

## e-acute — É

- IPA: /e/
- Sound description: Close-mid front unrounded vowel. Compared to è/ê, the mouth spreads more horizontally (as in a slight smile) and the tongue sits slightly higher and tenser. Voiced, oral, continuous.
- Zhuyin: ㄟ
- Zhuyin source note: Genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy source — abconline.com.tw gives "[e]...唸作「ㄟ」，嘴型似微笑" (pronounced ㄟ, mouth shaped like a smile). 法語鸚鵡螺/French Nautilus (Emily YO's dedicated Taiwanese French-pronunciation course) gives a related comparison, "é/er/ez [e] → ㄝㄧ（長音）," and its teacher explicitly contrasts é vs è by mouth shape in the course's own comment thread: "é [e] 嘴巴偏向左右開" (mouth opens more side-to-side) vs "è [ɛ] 嘴巴上下張得較開" (mouth opens more up-and-down). abconline's single-symbol ㄟ is used as the primary answer here since it maps to one clean bopomofo vowel; French Nautilus's compound ㄝㄧ is noted as an alternate, equally genuine Taiwanese comparison.

## c-cedilla — Ç

- IPA: /s/ (the cedilla has no independent sound of its own — it forces the preceding c to be read as /s/)
- Sound description: Same voiceless alveolar fricative as plain s — tongue tip near the ridge behind the upper teeth, air hisses through, no vocal cord vibration. The cedilla is purely a spelling device: it appears only under c, and only before a, o, u, to show that the c should be read as /s/ instead of the /k/ those vowels would otherwise trigger.
- Zhuyin: ㄙ
- Zhuyin source note: Same genuine Taiwanese-pedagogy source as plain s — abconline.com.tw's comparison of [s] to ㄙ applies directly, since ç always represents exactly the /s/ sound. French Nautilus / Emily YO's dedicated Taiwanese accented-letters course also explicitly confirms the function: "cédille... 強制把 c 用 [s] 發音" (the cedilla forces c to be read as [s]), giving "ça [sa]" as its example.
- Example word(s): ça, garçon

## a-grave — À
